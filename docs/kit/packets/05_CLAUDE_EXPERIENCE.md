## LiveKit and ElevenLabs

Yes, they can be part of later connected voice and demo asset production, with clear boundaries. LiveKit carries media; an ordinary Android SIM call cannot expose both sides of its audio to this app. A carrier/SIP route needs a reachable home device for local inference. When it cannot reach the device, the service must honestly queue/record or state unavailable. No cloud LLM/ASR/TTS fallback in the strict local runtime.

Use Carther's ElevenLabs credits for reviewed prerecorded Swahili assets if his account's output rights fit. Put keys in server-side secret storage or a private asset-generation environment, never in Android, git, room messages or recorded terminal output. A key is not needed to finish Phase 1. Do not ask him to paste it into the room.

Choose a Swahili-supporting model and compatible endpoint explicitly; current LiveKit docs retire ElevenLabs from LiveKit Inference while allowing the direct account plugin. Default examples are not proof of language coverage. Enterprise on-device ElevenLabs is proprietary and separate from normal API credits. Local MIT/Apache dynamic speech needs its own device gate; use Chatterbox/Whisper candidates only after the complete asset lineage and actual performance are checked.

Voice edge cases: partial readback, interruption, silence, background radio, negation, code-switching, prices/dates, 8 kHz line audio, repeated DTMF, spoofed caller ID and shared-phone owner identification. Never turn an uncertain transcript or generic 'yes' into approval. Do not keep listening indefinitely or record without an explicit consent flow.
