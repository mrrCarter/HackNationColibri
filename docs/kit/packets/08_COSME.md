# Cosme — buyer, distribution and economics packet

For Cosme de Ranieri. Recommended from your M&A/consulting background in the original profiles supplied by Carther. Your lane produces decisions and a usable economics model, not only a pitch deck.

## Your contribution

Test whether a cooperative/operator will pay for this exact workflow and whether the costs permit a sustainable service. Own contrib/cosme. Keep the product focused on learning from feedback and an approved follow-up rather than expanding into a complete tourism marketplace before proof.

Buyer hypothesis: a cooperative pays setup/training plus a per-active-operator subscription; a financed shared/home device is a later deployment option. Keep device cost, installation, telecom, support and software separate. The cooperative's sponsorship is not blanket access to Noor's private customers. Gifted ElevenLabs/Azure credits are experimental subsidies and need replacement costs in the model.

## Concrete work

1. Build a short interview script for one operator and one cooperative buyer. Ask about their current feedback workflow, who reads/ translates it, actual phone availability, signal, time spent, missed follow-ups, private information and purchasing authority. Ask for evidence of recent behavior before hypothetical enthusiasm.
2. Compare the current guide/daughter/paper/WhatsApp/template workflow with the proposed one. Document what a simple digital listing already solves. A listing, reseller reach and manual booking tool are real substitutes.
3. Build a small unit-economics sheet/CSV with three clearly labeled pricing hypotheses and break-even calculations. Use verified country-specific two-way SMS/call rates only after a target country/provider is identified. Leave unknown prices as unknown; don't fill them with invented averages.
4. Write a paid-pilot proposal: one cooperative, a small set of operators, setup owner, device/telecom responsibilities, success criteria, support cap, consent/export policy and a clear renewal decision. Proposed five operators is an experiment size, not evidence of current customers.

## Five forces, with useful answers

Rivalry and substitutes are high: platforms and broad agents can add this feature, while a human/template tool may suffice. Entry barriers from open models are low. Buyers have constrained budgets and supplier power comes from telecom/device/voice dependencies. Potential defenses are measured reliability, locally reviewed task data and repeatable cooperative deployment; prove retention and paid distribution before calling them moats. Do not claim that Muse lacks human approval, or that all booking tools lack local-language/offline functions without direct evidence.
